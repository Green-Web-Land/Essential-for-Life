# Facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md) · [Curriculum](CURRICULUM.md)

## Prepare

Use the printed or locally displayed lessons and fictional cards. Read aloud or describe diagrams when useful. Accept spoken responses without recording. Do not request account handles, private messages, real screenshots, personal incidents or screen-time histories.

Tell learners that declining to share can be a successful decision. Ask for the reason and evidence, rather than rewarding certainty, speed or popularity. Keep answer guidance available for self-study.

## Discussion prompts

1. What task are you trying to finish?
2. What does a feed leave unknown?
3. Who could receive a copy beyond the intended audience?
4. What exactly did the person agree to?
5. Does the source support this claim in this context?
6. Is verification independent of the suspicious request?
7. Could a factual correction or disengagement meet the need?
8. What stopping point fits the chosen task?
9. Which questions concern credit, permission or promotion?
10. What remains uncertain in the final draft?

Use only mild fictional disagreements. Learners may skip a scenario and use the event-time example instead. Do not simulate harassment or ask anyone to investigate a scam. For a real serious concern, help the person find appropriate local support without turning it into class evidence.

## Final-project rubric

Review [Lesson 10](lessons/10-project.md). Mark Demonstrated or Revise and explain why.

| Criterion | Demonstrated when the learner… |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence | Uses B's corrected time/date and beginner information, with attribution; does not infer attendance from C. |
| Rights and context | Withholds C because its caption/context and reuse permission are unresolved. |
| Consent | Respects D's refusal to share the photo or personal story. |
| Promotion | Declines E or identifies the need for honest, clear disclosure and further checks; invents no positive testimonial. |
| Audience and conduct | Proposes a limited audience, acknowledges copying limits and avoids targeting others. |
| Attention and judgement | Sets a finish condition, identifies an open question and explains the choice to share, revise or wait. |

All six are the instructional completion target, with revision allowed. This rubric is not a validated assessment or a professional credential. Another answer can pass when its reasoning meets the criteria, including a well-explained decision not to share anything.

## Feedback

Name a specific gap and a useful next step: “Card C shows last year's event. Revise the claim about this year's attendance.” Do not infer a learner's personality or mental health from an answer.

## Adaptation

Retain the [licence notices](LICENSE.md), label adaptations and review sources for changing services. Child-focused adaptations need separate audience and safeguarding review. Presence of alt text or a paper route does not prove accessibility conformance; check your actual delivery format and learner needs.
