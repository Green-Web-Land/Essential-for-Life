# 7. Disagree and set boundaries

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Choose a response that addresses an issue without escalating harm.

Participation can include asking a question, correcting a claim, thanking someone or choosing not to reply. A disagreement about an event time does not require a judgement about another person's character. Address the specific point, offer evidence and leave room for correction.

A useful response could say, “The organiser updated the time today; here is the relevant notice.” It need not repeat insults or expose personal details. Avoid inviting others to target someone, even when you believe they are wrong. Public shaming can add harm without resolving the original issue.

Distinguish an ordinary disagreement from a situation involving threats or repeated harassment. Disengaging is a valid choice. Services may offer mute, block and report controls, but their effects and reporting processes vary. Do not promise that reporting guarantees removal or that blocking ends all contact.

If handling a real serious incident, seek appropriate local support and consider evidence requirements before changing access or deleting material. Do not put yourself at risk to collect evidence. An official Australian reporting guide, for example, advises getting its guidance before blocking in its adult-abuse process; that is not a universal procedure for all countries. Immediate danger needs local emergency help.

This course uses mild fictional disagreements, not real abuse screenshots or confrontation practice. Learners may use the neutral event-time scenario throughout.

## Worked example

Someone replies “You got the time wrong.” Sam checks the organiser's correction, then writes a factual response. If the conversation turns into repeated personal attacks, Sam can stop rather than continue defending every point.

## Try it — paper is enough

Rewrite this fictional response: “Only an idiot would believe that. Everyone should message you.” Keep the correction, remove the attack and the call for targeting.

## Answer and reasoning

For example: “The later organiser notice gives 14:00. The earlier post had 13:00.” No personal judgement or invitation to contact the other person is needed. Choosing not to respond is also valid.

## Use it somewhere else

Write one boundary sentence and one help route you would consider in a fictional escalating discussion. Do not name real accounts.
