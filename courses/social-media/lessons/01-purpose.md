# 1. Choose your purpose

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Choose a useful goal and an appropriate way to meet it.

Social media lets people publish, discover and respond to content within networks or communities. A service may combine public posts, private messages, groups, advertising and recommendations. These features serve different purposes. You can use a community to learn about an event without wanting to build a public profile or argue in its comments.

Begin with a task: “Find the date of the neighbourhood repair afternoon.” Then choose the smallest useful activity. Reading the organiser's announcement may be enough. A phone call, a noticeboard or a direct conversation can sometimes meet the need better. Choosing not to use social media is a valid option, not a failure to learn.

Separate your goal from a popularity measure. Likes, shares and follower totals may show activity, but they do not establish whether a claim is correct or whether your task is complete. A helpful private exchange could meet your goal without attracting a public audience.

Decide what “finished” looks like before starting. It could be finding the date, writing down one unanswered question, or deciding that the source is insufficient. In this course, all tasks can be completed using fictional cards and paper. You do not need an account.

## Worked example

Sam wants to learn whether a fictional repair afternoon welcomes beginners. Sam's goal is an answer from the organiser, not collecting reactions. If the notice does not answer it, Sam can prepare an unsent question and stop.

## Try it — paper is enough

Match each goal to a possible route: A—learn an event time; B—show a sketch to one trusted friend; C—explore a hobby. For each, name one social-media route and one alternative.

## Answer and reasoning

A could use an organiser's post or a printed notice. B could use a limited message or an in-person conversation. C could use a hobby group or a library book. A good answer explains the audience and need; no route is best for everyone.

## Use it somewhere else

Write a fictional goal, a stopping point and one reason you might choose a different communication method.
