# 9. Create and credit responsibly

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Separate factual support, reuse permission and promotional disclosure.

Before sharing a new post, check its purpose, audience, accuracy and rights. Finding an image online does not itself grant reuse permission. Naming the creator is credit; it is not automatically permission. A licence may permit a particular use with conditions, while another use needs separate consideration. Copyright rules and exceptions depend on the jurisdiction and facts.

For this course, the simplest exercise uses your own fictional text and a described asset with explicit reuse terms. Keep the source, licence and any required change notice with the asset. Permission for an image does not necessarily resolve a depicted person's privacy or consent concerns.

If content promotes a product through a material relationship, make that relationship clear. Canada's Competition Bureau identifies paid arrangements and free products among connections that should be disclosed, and calls for prominent, understandable disclosure. A brand tag alone may be insufficient. This is Canadian general guidance, not a guarantee that one phrase meets every legal requirement worldwide.

Distinguish four kinds of rule: a principle such as respect; an enforceable law; a platform's policy; and a technical control such as an audience setting. A control does not establish legal permission, and a good intention does not fix a missing licence.

Use plain language, describe important visual information in text, and avoid claims beyond your evidence. A draft can be useful without being posted.

## Worked example

A fictional maker offers Sam a free tool in return for a post. Sam does not describe it as an independent purchase. Sam drafts a clear disclosure, limits statements to what is actually known and checks reuse rights for any image. No real promotion occurs.

## Try it — paper is enough

Asset A: unknown online photo. Asset B: fictional illustration explicitly offered under CC BY 4.0 with creator/source details. Which offers a clearer reuse route, and what else must you check?

## Answer and reasoning

B supplies a licence route; retain credit, licence link and required notices and identify changes. Check the scope and other rights rather than treating the label as a blanket guarantee. A's availability alone is insufficient.

## Use it somewhere else

Draft an original two-sentence event post using fictional facts and no image, then list the checks you avoided by choosing text only and those that still remain.
