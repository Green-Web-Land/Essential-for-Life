# 6. Check quality and handle mistakes

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Report a discrepancy and propose a bounded correction.

Adults 18+. All workplaces, people, policies and task timings in these exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no real workplace access or disclosure is required.

## Understand

Define a check before declaring work complete. Compare the output with the supplied requirements, not just its appearance. Useful checks can cover completeness, accuracy, version, permissions and whether the intended recipient can use the result.

When you find a mistake, distinguish the observed error from its possible effects. Say what you know, what you have not checked and who needs the information. Do not erase the record of an error to make a report look cleaner. Follow the actual correction procedure for the workplace; this fictional exercise grants no permission to modify live records.

A second look at your own work can catch errors, but it is not independent verification. A separate reviewer needs the right inputs and criteria too.

## Worked example

A fictional list requires item codes A, B and C. Noor's draft contains A, B and B. Noor reports the duplicate and missing C, checks the supplied source, and prepares a corrected draft with the changed row identified. Noor does not claim the source inventory is correct beyond the exercise, or that the recipient has approved the replacement.

## Practise

The requirement is “include the date and three required headings; preserve the supplied contact number.” A draft omits the date and changes one digit. Write a finding, a proposed correction and one recheck. All data is fictional; no call should be made.

## Suggested reasoning

Finding: the date is absent and the contact number differs from the supplied source. Correction: insert the supplied date and restore the number in a new draft using the permitted editing process. Recheck those fields and the three headings. Do not test the number by contacting an unknown person. Tell the reviewer which version supersedes the earlier draft.

## Try a new situation

An error may have reached a recipient, but you do not know whether they used it. Write a factual notification that preserves this uncertainty and asks the responsible person to coordinate correction.
