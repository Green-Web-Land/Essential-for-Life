# 1. Understand your role

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Separate assigned work, authority and missing information.

Adults 18+. All workplaces, people, policies and task timings in these exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no real workplace access or disclosure is required.

## Understand

A role describes work you are expected to contribute. It does not automatically give permission to use every system, speak for the organization or change another person's work. When starting a task, distinguish the outcome you own from the decisions somebody else owns.

Use a simple role note: expected output, people you work with, available resources, decision authority and questions still unanswered. A written job description may help, but it does not answer every practical question. Ask where current instructions and training are kept. CCOHS orientation guidance supports clarifying responsibilities and seeking help when procedures are unclear; it is not proof that this course replaces workplace training.

In this course, “working well” includes accurate reporting, respect and asking for the support needed to do a task. It does not mean silently accepting unsafe work or unlimited demands.

## Worked example

At fictional Maple Desk, Noor is asked to prepare a draft stock summary. The supplied role note permits reading the training inventory and creating a draft. Only supervisor Pat may approve purchase orders. Noor can summarize a shortage but cannot turn that finding into an order. A useful question is, “Who reviews the draft, and where should I flag missing inventory information?”

## Practise

A fictional visitor-desk role permits greeting visitors and calling the host. It does not permit issuing access badges. A visitor says they are late and asks you to let them in. Identify one authorized action, one action outside the stated role and a question for the responsible contact.

## Suggested reasoning

Calling the host is authorized; issuing a badge or bypassing access checks is not. Ask who can handle access under the supplied procedure. Do not infer authorization from urgency, clothing or confidence. This paper exercise does not tell you how any real building's access process works.

## Try a new situation

A team asks a new employee to “handle the invoice.” List two meanings that need clarification, such as checking details versus approving payment. Check that your answer does not assume authority to spend money.
