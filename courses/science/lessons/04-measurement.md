# 4. Measurement and records

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Preserve units, method and uncertainty when recording a value.

Adults 18+. All named people, examples, role cards and datasets are fictional. Use paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

A useful measurement record includes the quantity, value, unit, method and relevant conditions. State what the instrument or supplied record can distinguish. Extra decimal places do not create extra information.

NIST distinguishes closeness to the measured quantity's value from agreement among repeated readings. Consistent readings may share a bias. Measurement uncertainty is not simply a synonym for a mistake or the observed range; evaluating it can require several sources of information.

Here we use supplied display readings, not actual instruments. Keep original entries and document corrections so another reader can follow the change. Never invent missing units or silently replace an inconvenient value.

## Worked example

Fictional display records are 12 cm, 13 cm and 12 cm. Their observed range is 13 − 12 = 1 cm. This describes these readings, not a full uncertainty interval or the true length. Recording 12.000 cm would imply detail the supplied display did not provide.

## Practise

An invented row says “distance = 18” with no unit; another says “time = 5 s.” Which can you interpret as written? Write a correction request for the incomplete row.

## Suggested reasoning

The time row includes seconds. The distance row lacks a unit and needs clarification from its original record or author. Do not assume centimetres because another lesson used them. A correction should preserve the original and state the verified unit and reason once known.

## Try a new situation

Three identical fictional readings appear in a table. Does that establish accuracy? Check: agreement alone does not rule out a shared measurement bias; ask about the method and reference checks.
