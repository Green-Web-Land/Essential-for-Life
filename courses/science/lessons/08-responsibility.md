# 8. Research responsibly

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Recognise integrity and permission questions before collecting data.

Adults 18+. All named people, examples, role cards and datasets are fictional. Use paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

Honest records, clear source credit and respect for people matter throughout research. The Canadian Tri-Agency responsible-conduct framework addresses record keeping, accurate attribution and misleading manipulation within its stated agency/institutional scope. It is not a worldwide rulebook.

For research within the relevant Canadian TCPS 2 policy scope, consent and research ethics review have requirements that depend on the project. The policy describes consent as voluntary, informed and continuing, and addresses review before applicable recruitment or data collection. Do not decide an exemption from this lesson. Consult the responsible institution or qualified adviser before a real project.

Our activities use invented data openly labelled as fiction. Creating a teaching example is different from presenting invented numbers as real research. No participant recruitment, private-data collection, animal work or hazardous investigation is part of this course.

## Worked example

Fictional writer Ari wants to remove an unusual value because the graph looks untidy. Ari should retain the record and investigate whether there is a documented reason for exclusion. If a justified exclusion is made, report the rule and its effect; appearance is not a scientific reason.

## Practise

A fictional classmate proposes collecting classmates' health information tomorrow, saying “They can sign a form, so nothing else is needed.” Identify two concerns and a course-appropriate alternative.

## Suggested reasoning

A form alone does not settle informed participation, privacy or applicable ethics review. Do not collect the information for this activity. Use the supplied fictional records and, for any real research proposal, ask the responsible institution about requirements before proceeding.

## Try a new situation

A fictional paragraph uses someone else's figure with no credit. What needs checking beyond adding a name? Check source, permission/licence, required notices and whether adaptation is allowed; attribution alone does not grant reuse rights.
