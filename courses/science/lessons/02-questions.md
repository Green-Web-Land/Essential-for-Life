# 2. Ask an investigable question

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Turn a broad curiosity into a question linked to evidence.

Adults 18+. All named people, examples, role cards and datasets are fictional. Use paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

A question becomes more manageable when it names what is being compared or described, the outcome of interest and the relevant setting. “Which material is best?” lacks a criterion. “Which supplied material record shows the smallest change in length after the stated test?” identifies an outcome.

Decide what information could distinguish possible answers. A measurable question can still be poorly designed, unethical or impossible with the available resources. Narrowing a question does not authorize an experiment.

In this course, study only the printed fictional records. You may propose a next check on paper, including the information and qualified supervision it would need. Do not conduct the proposed test.

## Worked example

Fictional Ada asks, “Are all ramps the same?” A narrower paper question is: “In these records, how do travel distances compare between two surface labels at the same stated starting height?” This says which records and outcome matter; it does not claim the surface caused a difference.

## Practise

Rewrite “Which container is better?” using this invented context: records for containers A and B show temperature change over a stated interval. Name one further detail you need before comparing them.

## Suggested reasoning

One answer: “Which container record shows the smaller temperature change over the same interval under comparable conditions?” Starting temperature, amount of material and surrounding conditions are among the missing details. A smaller change only answers the chosen criterion, not every meaning of better.

## Try a new situation

New fictional topic: two pages have different print sizes. Form a question about supplied measurements of letter height without asking people to read or disclosing eyesight. Check: distinguish physical letter measurement from a claim about readability.
