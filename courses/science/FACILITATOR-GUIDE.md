# Becoming a Scientist — facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Teach reasoning with the supplied fiction. Do not turn the activities into live experiments, surveys or tests of participants. No private experience or workplace information is needed.

## Session approach

Read the goal, discuss the example, allow an answer, then compare with suggested reasoning. Ask which observation supports a statement and what remains unknown. An answer that identifies a genuine gap can be stronger than an invented conclusion.

Offer large text, a spoken response or a paper table where useful. Participants may skip an example or use another supplied fictional task. Do not treat a skipped activity or arithmetic difficulty as lack of scientific potential. Check understanding of units and sums separately from interpretation.

Keep original fictional values visible when correcting calculations. Discuss why a correct average does not repair an inadequate design. For ethics and career questions, use the source notes and actual institutional guidance rather than deciding eligibility or permission.

## Final project rubric

Lesson 10 has six tasks. Award 0 for missing or contradictory reasoning, 1 for partly supported reasoning with a material gap, or 2 for clear reasoning consistent with the supplied record. **Maximum: 12 points.** This is feedback on a paper exercise, not research authorization or a professional pass mark.

| Task | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| 1: arithmetic | Correct totals, means and ranges for A, B and C, with units. |
| 2: comparison | Chooses the bounded claim, identifies the height confound and explains remaining limits of A versus C. |
| 3: distinction | Gives two actual supplied observations and labels an explanation as tentative. |
| 4: design | Names comparable conditions, missing methods and individual records without claiming a test occurred. |
| 5: report | Separates question, evidence, limitation and proposed next check. |
| 6: integrity | Distinguishes labelled teaching fiction from invented data presented as actual research. |

Task 1 key: A total 66, mean 22, range 4 cm; B total 90, mean 30, range 4 cm; C total 66, mean 22, range 6 cm. Each sum and mean carries centimetres; the count is three readings per group. The B–A mean difference is 8 cm. Do not call this a statistically significant effect.

Accept alternative reports that remain consistent with the evidence. For transfer, the changed object is a remaining confound. A lower score points to a lesson to revisit, not a judgement of intelligence or suitability for a career.

## Boundaries

No chemical, biological, electrical, animal or participant investigation is required. A learner's proposed real project needs appropriate guidance and permissions outside this course. Avoid asking learners to demonstrate the ramp examples.

AI assistance is optional. If used, treat its explanations and calculations as claims to check; never submit private research records without authorization. No learner pilot, formal accessibility assessment or independent professional review has been performed.
