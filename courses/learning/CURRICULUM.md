# How to Learn and Check Information — curriculum

[Course home](README.md)

## Purpose and audience

Adult beginners 18+. Choose a small task, examine a worked example, attempt a response, compare it with evidence and revise. No previous course required. The audience and objectives are editorial, not measured learner outcomes.

| Lesson | Objective |
|---|---|
| [1. Choose a useful goal](lessons/01-goal.md) | Turn a broad intention into a task you can check. |
| [2. Connect an explanation and an example](lessons/02-understand.md) | Explain why a worked answer follows from its rule. |
| [3. Try recall, then check](lessons/03-recall.md) | Use a short attempt to find what needs correction. |
| [4. Return to practice and vary the example](lessons/04-return.md) | Plan revisits without treating a schedule as a universal prescription. |
| [5. Use feedback to revise](lessons/05-feedback.md) | Turn a specific correction into a new attempt. |
| [6. Read a claim carefully](lessons/06-claim.md) | Identify the scope and evidence a sentence needs. |
| [7. Trace and compare sources](lessons/07-sources.md) | Distinguish repeated reporting from additional evidence. |
| [8. Read numbers and diagrams](lessons/08-numbers.md) | Compare rates using their denominators and retain causal limits. |
| [9. Explain uncertainty and check tools](lessons/09-tools.md) | Use help without treating an output as its own evidence. |
| [10. Learn, check and explain](lessons/10-project.md) | Revise a claim using a supplied source packet and a new example. |

## Learning route

Lessons 1–5 focus on practising and checking a skill. Lessons 6–9 focus on claims, sources, numbers and tools. Lesson 10 combines those strands with fully supplied fictional material.

Try the practice before reading the suggested reasoning when useful. You may consult the explanation whenever needed; simply distinguish supported practice from an unaided attempt. A spoken answer or table is acceptable. Adjust the size of the task instead of treating one format as compulsory.

The final six-task rubric offers up to 12 points for this exercise. It is not a credential, intelligence score or prediction of later performance. Specific uncertainties can be part of a good answer.

No external account, personal example or real-world research participant is required. No timing norm or universal study schedule is imposed.
