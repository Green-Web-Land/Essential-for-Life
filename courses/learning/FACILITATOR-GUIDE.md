# How to Learn and Check Information — facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Offer the supplied fictional material without requesting real scores, diagnoses or personal difficulties. Accept written, spoken or tabular responses. A learner may consult a reference, use a calculator, take a break or reduce the task size.

## Facilitation

Ask which rule or evidence supports an answer. Give specific feedback about the response rather than a judgement about the person. Keep a correction distinct from an insult. Invite a new attempt after clarifying the reference.

A confident answer is not automatically correct; a hesitant answer is not automatically wrong. Separate numerical accuracy from causal interpretation. Do not require a learner to agree with an unsupported claim to receive credit.

Illustrative schedules are not prescriptions. Research notes identify populations and tasks studied; do not claim this course has demonstrated the same effects.

## Final project rubric

Six tasks; score each 0 for missing or contradictory reasoning, 1 for partly supported reasoning with an important gap, or 2 for reasoning consistent with the supplied packet. Maximum **12 points**. This is learning feedback, not a credential or intelligence judgement.

| Task | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| 1: rule | Only card 6 with a stripe belongs in K; explains both conditions and why the others fail. |
| 2: rates | Original P 60%, corrected P 50%, Q 80%; corrected Q-minus-P gap 30 percentage points. |
| 3: sources | B repeats A, C corrects A, D's appendix is unavailable; no independent trial invented. |
| 4: wording | Uses corrected immediate-task rates without “best for everyone.” |
| 5: limits | Identifies self-selection and absent baseline or delayed evidence; does not infer lifelong causation. |
| 6: next step | Specifies a small revisit task, a check and a remaining uncertainty. A fictional learner is acceptable. |

Accept other appropriately bounded headlines and learning plans. A learner can identify more than one reason why the headline fails.

## Answer keys

- Lesson 1: a valid small goal names an action, material and check; no unique wording.
- Lesson 2: only 6 with a stripe belongs in K; transfer only 5 with a dot belongs in M.
- Lesson 3: Neri/circle, Tavo/square, Luma/triangle. These are invented labels.
- Lesson 4: 10 + 10 + 10 = 30 minutes; four 8-minute sessions total 32.
- Lesson 5: 7 with a stripe fails evenness; 10 with a stripe meets both conditions.
- Lesson 6: an immediate result among volunteers does not establish everyone or lifelong retention.
- Lesson 7: corrected count 10; B adds no independent measurement; unavailable appendix stays unverified.
- Lesson 8: 12/20 = 60%, 8/10 = 80%, gap 20 percentage points. 9/15 and 6/10 both equal 60%. 40% to 50% is +10 percentage points or +25% relative to 40%.
- Lesson 9: 12/20 is 60%; 9/15 is 60%; neither calculation proves universal success.
- Lesson 10: mini 3/5 = 60%. Corrected P 10/20 = 50%, Q 8/10 = 80%, gap 30 percentage points. P changes by −10 percentage points.
- Lesson 10 transfer: U 6/12 = 50%, V 9/15 = 60%, gap 10 percentage points; odd-and-dot rule accepts 5 with a dot, not 6 with a dot.

No personal scores need to be stored or published. No learner pilot, independent review or formal accessibility assessment.
