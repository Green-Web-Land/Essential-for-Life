# 9. Explain uncertainty and check tools

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Use help without treating an output as its own evidence.

Adults 18+. Supplied practice records are fictional. Use paper, speech or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

A calculator can help with arithmetic, but it will not decide whether you entered the correct denominator. A fluent explanation can still misread the question.

NIST's generative-AI profile describes confidently presented false content as a risk. Treat a generated answer and its references as things to check. This course requires no AI account; the output below is invented.

Use three statements: what the supplied evidence supports, what remains uncertain and what would help resolve it. You can report a precise calculation while remaining uncertain about its interpretation.

## Worked example

Invented tool answer: “12 out of 20 is 80%, so this method always works.” Recalculate: 12 ÷ 20 × 100 = 60%. The universal conclusion also exceeds the evidence, even after fixing the number.

## Practise

Check this invented output: “9 of 15 is 60%. Therefore everyone will succeed next time. A report proves it, but no report is supplied.” Identify what can be checked here and what cannot.

## Suggested reasoning

The arithmetic is correct. The prediction about everyone is unsupported. The missing report cannot be verified from this packet. A better answer separates the 60% sample result from unknown future performance.

## Try a new situation

A tool supplies a plausible-looking title and link. Is that enough to call its source checked? Check: no; the source must be accessible, relevant and actually support the claim. Do not upload private records to complete this exercise.
