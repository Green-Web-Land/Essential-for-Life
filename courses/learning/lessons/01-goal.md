# 1. Choose a useful goal

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Turn a broad intention into a task you can check.

Adults 18+. Supplied practice records are fictional. Use paper, speech or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

“Learn everything about computers” is a direction, but it does not tell you what to practise next. Choose a smaller action: explain a term, interpret a table or apply a rule to a new example.

For this course, write three parts: the task, the supplied material and the check. This is a planning aid, not a test of motivation or intelligence. You can make the task smaller or use a spoken response instead of writing.

Decide what evidence would show progress before doing the task. Time spent or a feeling of familiarity may be useful context, but neither alone demonstrates that you can apply an idea.

## Worked example

Broad aim: “Understand percentages.” Small goal: “Using the supplied table, explain how 3 out of 4 becomes 75%, then solve a different example and check the answer.” The task and check are visible.

## Practise

Rewrite “I want to be better at reading information” as one small task using a fictional notice. Include what you would inspect and how you would check your response. No real personal goal is required.

## Suggested reasoning

One answer: “Read the supplied notice, identify who wrote it and what it claims, then compare my answer with the source card.” Other small tasks are valid if they specify an action and an appropriate check. A goal need not include a timed deadline.

## Try a new situation

A learner can name a rule but cannot apply it. What might the next goal be? Check: apply that rule to one supplied new example and explain each step.
