# 2. Connect an explanation and an example

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Explain why a worked answer follows from its rule.

Adults 18+. Supplied practice records are fictional. Use paper, speech or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

A worked example shows both a result and a route to it. Ask what each step does and why it is allowed. Repeating the final words is different from explaining the connection.

The IES study guide recommends combining examples, practice and explanation. Its main audience is school educators; adopting a similar structure here does not establish that this adult course has been tested.

Use words, symbols or a table to explain the same idea. Choosing a comfortable format is allowed; it does not assign you a permanent learning category.

## Worked example

Fictional card rule: “A card belongs in tray K only when its number is even AND it has a stripe.” Card 4 with a stripe belongs in K: both conditions hold. Card 4 without a stripe does not; meeting only one condition is insufficient.

## Practise

Apply the rule to:
- Card 3 with a stripe.
- Card 6 with a stripe.
- Card 8 without a stripe.

Explain each answer. A stripe can be described in words; no colour or drawing is needed.

## Suggested reasoning

Only card 6 with a stripe belongs in K. Card 3 fails the even-number condition; card 8 fails the stripe condition. “AND” requires both, so a correct explanation names the relevant conditions rather than copying the example's number.

## Try a new situation

New rule: tray M requires an odd number AND a dot. Consider card 5 with a dot and card 6 with a dot. Check: only card 5 belongs in M. The labels changed; the two-condition reasoning remains.
