# 4. Return to practice and vary the example

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Plan revisits without treating a schedule as a universal prescription.

Adults 18+. Supplied practice records are fictional. Use paper, speech or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

Spacing means returning to material across separate sessions. Varying practice means changing examples or task types instead of repeating only one familiar item.

IES's research-project summary reports benefits of spacing in several studied tasks and also reports a task where a spacing effect was not found. Results depend on conditions. The schedule below is an illustration, not a validated recipe for everyone.

Choose manageable sessions and adjust when needed. Include something to attempt and check; merely leaving time between readings does not tell you what was learned.

## Worked example

Fictional plan: Monday 10 minutes with a worked rule, Wednesday 10 minutes attempting recall, Saturday 10 minutes applying the rule to a new example. Total planned time is 30 minutes. No particular gain is promised.

## Practise

Compare that plan with one 30-minute Monday session. What is the same and what differs? If Wednesday is missed, suggest a revision without increasing the required total time.

## Suggested reasoning

Both plans allocate 30 minutes, but distribute them differently. The record supplies plans, not outcomes, so it cannot prove one learner improved. One revision moves Wednesday's 10 minutes to Thursday, preserving the total. Other practical revisions are possible.

## Try a new situation

A new plan has four 8-minute sessions. Check: total 32 minutes, not 30. Explain why more scheduled minutes alone do not prove better understanding.
