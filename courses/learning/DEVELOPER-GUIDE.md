## Preserve the learning task

Keep explanations, attempts and suggested reasoning distinguishable. Do not require speed, public scores or personal disclosures. Let readers choose text, speech or equivalent representations where your application supports them; do not claim unsupported accessibility conformance.

Preserve every fictional label. Cedar Desk, its records and the group comparison are invented. Do not present them as measured evidence that this course works.

When changing a number, update tables, worked answers, diagrams and rubric together. Keep counts, denominators, percentages and percentage points distinct. C's correction replaces P's numerator only; it does not change the denominator or create an independent trial.

Retain research populations, dates and limits. Do not add fixed learning-style diagnoses, universal schedules, credential promises or guaranteed outcomes.

## Hosting and tools

The files are static and implement no accounts or collection of learner records. Assess the actual host separately; static content does not prove that hosting collects nothing. Added analytics, AI, assessment storage or child-facing adaptations fall outside the reviewed package.

No AI account or external browsing is needed to complete the exercises. If optional tools are added, preserve source-checking steps and a usable route without them. Do not send private learner records to a service without separately addressing the actual practice.
