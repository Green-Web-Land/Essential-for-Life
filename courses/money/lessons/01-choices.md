# 1. Money and choices

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Identify a goal, a trade-off and information still needed.

Money can help us exchange goods and services, compare prices and set resources aside. A money decision also has a context: housing, health, family responsibilities, access and time. The same expense may be essential for one person and optional for another.

Start with a goal that describes a need, not a product. “Travel reliably to work” leaves room to compare routes; “buy this vehicle” has already chosen one. Ask what problem needs solving, when it matters and which resources are actually available.

A trade-off means that choosing one use of limited resources can leave less for another. It does not mean every problem can be solved by giving up small comforts. Sometimes income is insufficient for necessities, or options are constrained. A useful plan makes the gap visible rather than judging the person.

Distinguish a fact, an estimate and a preference. A supplied bill can establish an amount due; next month's variable income may be an estimate; choosing convenience over another feature is a preference. Uncertainty belongs in the plan.

All people, figures and offers in this course are fictional. Dollar amounts are Canadian dollars (CAD), chosen to teach arithmetic, not to describe typical living costs or current market prices. No real account information is needed.

## Worked example

Alex's goal is to replace worn work shoes. Before choosing a purchase, Alex notes the required fit, the timing and the money available after existing commitments. The exercise does not assume a cheaper item will meet the need.

## Try it — paper is enough

For a fictional person deciding how to get to an appointment, list a goal, two possible routes and one missing fact. Do not use your own finances.

## Answer and reasoning

A goal could be arriving on time with an accessible route. Public transport and a ride from an available trusted person are possibilities, not guaranteed options. The timetable, accessibility or availability may still need checking.

## Use it somewhere else

Rewrite a fictional product wish as a need or goal. Explain why choosing to wait for information can be a reasonable result.

## Sources and limits
