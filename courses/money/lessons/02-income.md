# 2. Income and payment timing

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Calculate take-home income from supplied figures and distinguish averages from cash received.

Gross pay is pay before deductions. Net pay is the amount after deductions. A spending plan should not treat a gross-pay figure as cash that is all available to spend. The meaning and amount of deductions depend on the pay arrangement and applicable rules.

Our fictional pay slip gives gross pay CAD 1,600 and total deductions CAD 320, so net pay is CAD 1,280. These are supplied teaching numbers, not a tax calculation or a normal deduction rate. Two such payments in one specified month give CAD 2,560 net income. Twice-monthly and every-two-weeks schedules are different; use actual dates rather than assuming they always produce the same monthly count.

Income can vary. Three fictional monthly amounts of CAD 1,400, CAD 1,800 and CAD 1,600 average CAD 1,600. That average does not guarantee the next month's income or make cash arrive before a bill is due. Label estimated receipts and consider what changes if they arrive later or are lower.

A loan receipt brings cash but creates an obligation; it is not earned income. Moving money between your own accounts also does not create new income. Avoid counting the same receipt twice.

For this lesson, use only the supplied pay slip and amounts. Do not calculate personal taxes, benefit eligibility or payroll entitlements from these examples.

## Worked example

Alex lists the two expected CAD 1,280 payments on their dates instead of entering CAD 3,200 gross pay as spendable cash. Alex keeps a separate note about the deductions without guessing their legal basis.

## Try it — paper is enough

A second fictional slip gives gross CAD 900 and deductions CAD 135. Find net pay. Then explain why a three-month income average is not a promise of next month's cash.

## Answer and reasoning

CAD 765 = 900 − 135. An average summarises the supplied past figures; the next receipt's amount and timing remain separate questions.

## Use it somewhere else

Make a paper income card with fields for amount, date, source and whether it is confirmed or estimated. Use fictional data.
