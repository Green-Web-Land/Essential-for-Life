# 3. Expenses and priorities

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Classify costs and allow for periodic bills without counting them twice.

Fixed costs tend to stay the same over a chosen period; variable costs can change. These labels do not tell you whether an expense is important. A fixed subscription may be optional, while variable food or heating costs can be essential.

Periodic costs arrive less often than the budget period. A fictional annual CAD 240 bill corresponds to CAD 20 per month if spread evenly across twelve months. A CAD 90 bill every three months corresponds to CAD 30 per month. Those averages are planning amounts, not a change to the actual payment dates.

Setting aside money for a future bill is a transfer or allocation. When you later pay the bill from that reserve, do not treat both the monthly set-asides and the payment as new spending in the same total. Be clear whether your table describes spending, cash leaving an account, or money reserved for later use.

A list should name assumptions. Does a food amount include household supplies? Are fees included in a payment? Is a repair cost known or estimated? A precise-looking total can still omit a category.

There is no universal essential/optional list for every household. A communication service may support work, disability access or caregiving. Look for missing information before recommending a cut in a fictional plan.

## Worked example

Alex labels a CAD 240 annual bill “CAD 20 monthly planning allocation; full bill still due annually.” If only part of the year remains before the due date, that simple twelve-month allocation may not build enough in time.

## Try it — paper is enough

A fictional bill of CAD 180 is due every six months. Find its even monthly allocation. Why might that allocation be insufficient if the next bill is due next month and nothing is reserved?

## Answer and reasoning

CAD 30 = 180 ÷ 6. With no existing reserve and only one month left, setting aside CAD 30 would leave CAD 150 still needed for that bill. Timing matters as well as the average.

## Use it somewhere else

Create one fictional fixed, variable and periodic cost. Explain which fact you would need before deciding whether any could be changed.
