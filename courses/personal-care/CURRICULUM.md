# Everyday Personal Care — curriculum

[Course home](README.md)

Adult beginners 18+. Complete the full course or choose a topic; there is no timed requirement. Use supplied fictional scenarios on paper. Reading support, larger text and spoken responses can be offered with the learner's agreement. No personal care task must be performed.

| Lesson | Learning goal |
|---|---|
| [1. Care, dignity and realistic routines](lessons/01-routines.md) | Design a manageable routine without judging a person's worth. |
| [2. Supplies, labels and access](lessons/02-supplies.md) | Identify missing information before using a product or planning assistance. |
| [3. Hand hygiene](lessons/03-hands.md) | Arrange the general handwashing sequence and explain its scope. |
| [4. Body hygiene and bathing](lessons/04-bathing.md) | Plan preparation and recognise when a routine needs individual guidance. |
| [5. Everyday oral care](lessons/05-mouth.md) | Explain basic adult guidance and identify an individual-care question. |

| [7. Clothing, towels and bedding](lessons/07-clothing.md) | Build a cleaning plan that respects labels and actual facilities. |
| [8. Rest, comfort and consistency](lessons/08-rest.md) | Choose a practical routine adjustment without promising a cure. |
| [9. Recognise limits and ask for help](lessons/09-help.md) | Write a focused question and choose a suitable kind of support. |

## Learning route

Lessons 1–2 establish dignity, planning and labels. Lessons 3–8 introduce bounded general information and logistical decisions. Lesson 9 distinguishes practical support from qualified care. Lesson 10 combines those ideas in a fictional project.

A learner should be able to explain a source-supported general principle, identify unknowns, preserve individual instructions and ask a respectful question. These are intended learning goals, not validated health outcomes. No completion certificate or professional qualification is offered.

## Materials and boundaries

Paper or an equivalent accessible way to answer is sufficient. The fictional labels are written in the lessons; do not bring household chemicals, sharp tools, real health documents or images of a person's body. Any activity can be skipped or replaced by discussing a neutral label or access problem.

General adult guidance is not a regimen for a particular condition. The dated [sources](SOURCES.md) identify the Canadian, UK and US references. [Facilitator guidance](FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md) explains feedback and limits.
