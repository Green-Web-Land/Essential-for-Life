# Everyday Personal Care — developer guide

[Course home](README.md)

Copy the complete course directory, including lessons, PNG/SVG images, sources and licence. Preserve relative paths or update and test them consistently. Provide a route back to your own catalogue if you relocate the course home.

## Preserve meaning

Keep the adult audience, fictional examples, optional participation, general-information limits and source dates visible. Do not turn questions into mandatory health disclosures, diagnoses, treatment recommendations or symptom-checker outputs. Do not remove the context limiting dry-skin guidance or portray a withdrawn textile standard as current law.

The five diagrams have adjacent text equivalents. Preserve those equivalents and alt text when changing the presentation. Do not rely on colour alone. SVG sources are included for layout changes; recheck labels and consistency with lesson text after any adaptation.

## Integration and data

The supplied files are static educational content. They do not themselves implement accounts, analytics, confidential messaging or health-data storage. Any host application may add its own behaviour; inspect and disclose that behaviour rather than inheriting a “no collection” claim from these files.

No AI service is required. If you add AI, assessments, live assistance or personal-data features, assess those changes separately before use. An AI explanation is not qualified clinical care. Do not present publisher Issues or email as a care service.

## Reuse

Free commercial reuse and adaptation are permitted for rights held under [CC BY 4.0](LICENSE.md). Retain attribution and source/licence links, identify changes and do not imply endorsement. Linked third-party pages retain their own terms. A translation or changed audience needs a fresh factual and suitability review; an adult label cannot make child-facing content appropriate by itself.

Before distributing an adaptation, check every relative link, image, factual instruction, scope statement and source mapping. Course completion is not a professional qualification.
