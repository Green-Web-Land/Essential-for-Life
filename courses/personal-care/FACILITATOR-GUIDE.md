# Everyday Personal Care — facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Facilitate reasoning about fiction. Do not assess participants' bodies, hygiene, symptoms or fitness to care for another person. You need not request anyone's health history, home situation, culture or explanation for skipping a topic.

## Before and during a lesson

Explain that all required work is on paper and participants may pass. Offer readable text, a spoken answer or a neutral alternative such as planning storage for an invented item. Do not ask learners to bring chemicals, grooming tools, personal photographs or health records. Do not demonstrate transfers, treatment or physical care.

Read the goal, discuss the worked example, let learners answer, and then compare reasoning. An answer can correctly identify missing information instead of resolving it. Respect different preferences; do not rank people by appearance, spending or routine complexity.

If someone asks an individual health question, acknowledge it and explain the limit of this activity. Direct the question to an appropriate qualified professional rather than diagnosing or prescribing. Do not promise confidentiality, eligibility or access that you cannot ensure. Do not solicit sensitive information in GitHub Issues or course discussions. Use local emergency assistance for an actual emergency, not the course response channel.

## Feedback for lessons

Each lesson supplies suggested reasoning and a transfer check. Accept alternative answers that respect the given facts, existing instructions and safety boundaries. Ask “Which fact supports that?” or “What still needs clarification?” Avoid rewarding guessed certainty.

Examples:
- An unreadable bottle label remains a gap even if someone recognises the colour.
- A planned activity may be incomplete because the necessary space is unavailable.
- A person's preference does not establish a diagnosis.
- A paper sequence does not demonstrate real-world skill.

## Final project rubric

Use lesson 10's six tasks. The seven criteria below separate the factual and uncertainty parts of task 3. Award 0 for absent or contradictory reasoning, 1 for partly supported reasoning with an important gap, or 2 for clear reasoning consistent with the supplied facts. Maximum: **14 points**. The score describes this exercise only; there is no health or qualification pass mark.

| Criterion | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| Task 1: critique | Identifies all five distinct problems in plan A without inventing a condition. |
| Task 2: limits | Explains why B is preferable and names arrangements or advice still needed. |
| Task 3: facts | Gives three accurate supplied facts. |
| Task 3: unknowns | Gives three genuine unknowns without diagnosing Sam. |
| Task 4: planning | Connects proposed actions to prerequisites and focused questions. |
| Task 5: sequence | Uses lesson 3's source-supported sequence, coverage and timing without claiming demonstrated technique. |
| Task 6: respect | Asks a clear question without assigning unwanted help or requiring private disclosure. |

Review gaps by revisiting the relevant lesson. A learner who skips can review an alternative fictional task without a score. For the transfer task, notice Pat's changed schedule rather than copying Sam's access problems.

## Limits

This edition has not undergone a learner pilot, clinical validation or independent professional assessment. Facilitators should not convert it into treatment advice or use the rubric to judge a person's real-life care.
