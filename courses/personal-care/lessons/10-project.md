# 10. Build a fictional care plan

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Compare routines, identify gaps and preserve individual guidance.

Adults 18+. All people, labels and scenarios used in exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no physical demonstration or personal disclosure is required. You may skip a scenario.

## Understand

Use only the supplied fiction. Your answer is a planning exercise, not a care plan for a real person. A complete answer can say “unknown” and identify who could clarify it. A long list of products is not necessarily a better plan.

Separate activities supported by general guidance, logistical arrangements needing agreement and questions requiring individual advice. Use the facilitator rubric to review your reasoning; its score does not measure health or personal worth.

## Worked example

Mini-example: a fictional learner has a towel but no confirmed access to the washroom. Listing the towel as available is accurate; listing bathing as fully arranged is not. The missing access needs clarification before claiming the plan is workable.

## Practise

Fictional adult Sam shares a bathroom, has a confirmed evening slot, cannot comfortably read small labels and has existing dental instructions. A sweater label says “hand wash; dry flat”; a drying surface has not been arranged. Sam says a nail has become painful. No other medical details are supplied.

Plan A: replace the dental instructions with an online routine; use an unlabelled donated bottle; tumble-dry the sweater; follow a nail-treatment video; promise perfect sleep after one evening routine.
Plan B: keep the existing instructions while asking about any difficulty following them; obtain readable product information; check washing/drying arrangements; ask a qualified professional about the nail; plan rest without promising results.

1. Identify five problems with A.
2. Explain why B is a better starting point but not yet complete.
3. List three supplied facts and three unknowns.
4. Propose a short routine-planning table with action, prerequisite and next question.
5. Add the general handwashing sequence from lesson 3 as a paper exercise.
6. Write one respectful question about assistance or qualified care without diagnosing Sam.

## Suggested reasoning

A overrides existing advice, guesses bottle contents, contradicts the drying label, promotes an unassessed treatment and promises a sleep outcome. B respects limits but still needs product identification, access details and clinical guidance.

Facts include the evening slot, small-label difficulty and sweater directions. Unknowns include the bottle's identity, appropriate drying space and the cause/urgency of nail pain. Do not invent a diagnosis.

Possible table: obtain readable instructions / reliable source needed / who can supply the original text? Plan sweater care / suitable wash and dry setup needed / where is dry-flat space available? Clarify dental routine / existing advice retained / what adaptation should be discussed? Seek nail guidance / qualified contact needed / which local professional can assess this concern? This does not schedule a treatment or guarantee access.

Paper handwashing sequence: wet, soap, rub all surfaces for at least 20 seconds, rinse and dry with a clean towel; use the towel for the tap. Ask what assistance Sam wants rather than assigning a helper or requiring private disclosure.

## Try a new situation

New fictional setting: Pat has readable labels and laundry facilities, but a changing work schedule. Identify one planning question about rest and one reason not to copy Sam's whole routine. Check that you adapt to the supplied difference rather than assuming identical needs.
