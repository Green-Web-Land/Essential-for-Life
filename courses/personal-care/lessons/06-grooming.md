# 6. Hair, grooming and nails

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Distinguish ordinary preferences from questions requiring qualified care.

Adults 18+. All people, labels and scenarios used in exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no physical demonstration or personal disclosure is required. You may skip a scenario.

## Understand

Grooming choices can reflect comfort, culture and preference. Hair texture and scalp needs vary. AAD guidance recommends adapting washing to how dirty or oily hair becomes, handling hair gently and limiting excessive heat. This course does not assign one washing calendar to all hair types or diagnose scalp problems.

For nails, AAD recommends keeping them clean and dry and avoiding biting or removing cuticles. Its guidance directs nail changes, swelling or pain to professional assessment, with particular attention to people with diabetes or poor circulation. We do not teach cutting tools, treating ingrown nails or removing damaged skin.

Use the fictional examples to practise asking the right question, not judging appearance. No photograph, touching or grooming demonstration is required.

## Worked example

Fictional Lee and Kai have different hair types and routines. A comparison based only on who washes more often cannot establish who cares for their hair better. A useful question concerns their actual hair/scalp needs, preferences and any existing guidance.

## Practise

A fictional person reports a painful nail and wants to copy a video showing how to dig it out. Choose a more appropriate next step and explain what the course cannot decide.

## Suggested reasoning

Seek qualified assessment rather than attempt the video procedure. The course cannot identify the cause, choose treatment or determine urgency from this brief story. Avoid presenting ordinary grooming as a solution to pain.

## Try a new situation

A fictional learner prefers not to remove body hair. Explain why this course does not require changing that preference.
