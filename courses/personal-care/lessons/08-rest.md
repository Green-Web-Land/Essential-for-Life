# 8. Rest, comfort and consistency

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Choose a practical routine adjustment without promising a cure.

Adults 18+. All people, labels and scenarios used in exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no physical demonstration or personal disclosure is required. You may skip a scenario.

## Understand

Rest belongs in personal care, but a calendar cannot diagnose or treat a sleep problem. PHAC's sleep information identifies regular sleep/wake timing and reducing noise as habits that may help. The referenced page dates from 2019; its survey statistics are not used as current figures here.

A useful planning question is what can realistically change. Shift work, caring duties and shared accommodation may limit choices. Do not assume every person controls the room or can adopt the same bedtime. A routine should not require changing medicines or adding sleep products without suitable professional advice.

Separate a planned rest opportunity from actual sleep. More scheduled time does not guarantee sleep quality. Persistent or concerning difficulties are appropriate questions for a healthcare professional.

## Worked example

Fictional Ren shares a home where a radio plays near the sleeping area. Ren can ask whether a quieter arrangement is possible. That is a proposed environmental change, not proof that noise explains every sleep difficulty.

## Practise

Compare two fictional plans: A promises that a strict new bedtime will cure tiredness; B proposes a feasible regular rest period, checks household constraints and leaves unresolved health questions for professional guidance. Explain the stronger reasoning.

## Suggested reasoning

B distinguishes a practical action from a medical outcome. A makes a promise the scenario cannot support. B still needs agreement about shared arrangements and may need individual guidance; it does not guarantee better sleep.

## Try a new situation

A fictional worker's schedule changes weekly. Write a question about feasible rest planning rather than assigning a universal bedtime. [PHAC sleep reference](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/healthy-living/canadian-adults-getting-enough-sleep-infographic.html).
