# 1. Care, dignity and realistic routines

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Design a manageable routine without judging a person's worth.

Adults 18+. All people, labels and scenarios used in exercises are fictional. Work on paper; no physical demonstration or personal disclosure is required. You may skip a scenario.

## Understand

Personal care includes keeping comfortable, looking after the body and organizing everyday supplies. It is not a competition in appearance. A person's clothing, disability or access to a bathroom does not tell you their character.

Start with a purpose and the practical situation. What is already working? Which supplies or spaces are available? What needs assistance or professional guidance? A routine can include regular activities and tasks done when needed; it need not be a rigid list with every activity performed at the same time.

Our planning method is an editorial exercise, not a clinical care plan. Keep any individual instructions from a qualified professional in view. If adapting them is difficult, ask for help rather than replacing them with an internet routine.

## Worked example

Fictional Ren shares a bathroom and has limited morning time. Ren lists existing care instructions, available supplies and a preferred private time. A useful plan confirms bathroom availability and puts supplies where Ren can reach them. “Wake earlier and do everything perfectly” does not solve an access problem.

## Practise

Fictional Alex has a reliable evening bathroom slot but unpredictable mornings. Write two practical questions and one routine-planning improvement. Do not remove a needed activity merely because it is inconvenient.

## Suggested reasoning

Ask what can be prepared ahead and which activities have timing instructions that need to be respected. One improvement is organizing supplies the evening before, with Alex's agreement. Do not assume all morning care can be moved to evening or that a shared space is always available.

## Try a new situation

Rewrite “Someone who misses a routine is lazy” as a question about barriers. Check: it should ask what made the routine difficult without demanding a personal explanation.
