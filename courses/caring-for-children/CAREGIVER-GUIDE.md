# Guide for adult learners and facilitators

[Course home](README.md)

Study one lesson at a time. Try the supplied exercise before reading its answer. Compare the reasoning, not just the wording. A response can be respectful even if the child remains unhappy; calm compliance does not establish good care.

For group study, use the fictional cases. Do not invite disclosures about children, require accounts of trauma, or ask participants to diagnose each other's families. Allow a pass without explanation. Do not record the discussion by default. If someone raises an actual protection concern, use the appropriate help route rather than debating it as a case study.

## Practise the distinction

Joining a child's world: asking about an imagined creature or what a game means to them. Keeping adult responsibility: stopping the game near a real hazard. Giving up responsibility: allowing danger to avoid disappointment. Taking over: replacing the child's harmless story with your own. The first two can occur together.

## Reflection without surveillance

Ask yourself whether you listened, kept feasible promises and offered appropriate choices. Do not create a scoring system for a child's affection, disclosures or obedience. You can complete the course entirely with imaginary situations. If you choose to reflect on actual care, keep identifying information out of public forums and AI services.

## Limits and support

This material does not qualify a person to provide professional childcare, medical treatment or child-protection investigation. Caregiver stress, persistent difficulty and health concerns may need appropriate support beyond education. Seek urgent help if safety is at risk. [Lesson 11](lessons/11-safety.md) identifies geographic limits and help routes.
