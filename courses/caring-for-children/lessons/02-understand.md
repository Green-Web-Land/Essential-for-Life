# 2. Understand before interpreting

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Separate observations from explanations you have invented.

## Explanation

Write mentally in two columns: what happened, and what you think it means. A lowered head does not by itself tell you whether someone is ashamed, tired, concentrating or avoiding bright light. Use a tentative question when useful; do not turn every movement into an interrogation.

One open invitation may help more than a stream of “why” questions. Let the child speak, point, draw, use their usual communication aid, or return later. Check your understanding in ordinary words. Do not secretly record a conversation to analyse it. The exercises use fictional information, not real family case notes.

Understanding is not extracting a confession. When there is a safety concern, use the help route in [lesson 11](11-safety.md), rather than using this activity to investigate.

![Observation; Possibility; Unknown](../images/facts.png)

Text equivalent: Observation: Said: Too loud; Use supplied information. Possibility: A quieter place may help; Offer, then check. Unknown: Why noise mattered; Do not invent a diagnosis.

## Worked example

Fictional: Ari leaves a board game. Supplied facts: Ari put down the cards and said, “Too loud.” The adult initially thinks, “Ari hates losing.” A better response is, “The noise bothered you? We can pause here.” Ari may correct that interpretation. Nothing in the facts establishes who was winning or why the noise mattered.

## Practice

Classify: A. Ari said “Too loud.” B. Ari wanted to spoil everyone's evening. C. Ari left the table. D. Ari has a disorder. E. Ari might prefer a quieter place. Which can support a tentative offer, and which would need information you do not have?

## Answers and checking

A and C are supplied observations. B and D are unsupported conclusions. E is a possibility, suitable for a low-pressure offer, not a fact. “Would somewhere quieter help?” checks it. Refusal of that offer does not prove B.

## Try independently

Fictional: a child says a school drawing is “wrong.” List two unknowns and one gentle invitation. Do not infer bullying, perfectionism or a diagnosis from this sentence.
