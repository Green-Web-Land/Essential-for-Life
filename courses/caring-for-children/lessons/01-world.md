# 1. Enter the child’s world

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Notice what matters to a child without giving up adult responsibility.

## Explanation

A small event can have a large place in a child's day. Begin with the situation the child is experiencing, rather than judging its importance by your adult priorities. “Become a child” here means curiosity and willingness to play; the adult still protects safety, keeps necessary commitments and manages their own conduct.

Our suggested sequence is notice, invite, and adjust. Notice something concrete. Invite a response without demanding one. Adjust when the child corrects your understanding. This is a practice framework, not a tested treatment or guarantee.

UNICEF describes attentive listening and shared enjoyment as ways to communicate with young children. [Source](https://www.unicef.org/eca/stories/how-communicate-effectively-your-young-child). An invitation can be declined. Comfortable distance, silence or looking elsewhere can belong in the conversation.

![Notice; Invite; Adjust](../images/world.png)

Text equivalent: Notice: Name what happened; Keep guesses tentative. Invite: Offer space to respond; Silence is an option. Adjust: Let the child correct you; Keep adult responsibility.

## Worked example

Fictional: Jo's paper spaceship tears before leaving for an appointment. “It's only paper” answers the adult's assessment, not Jo's concern. An alternative: “The wing tore. You worked on it. Shall we put it somewhere safe? We still need to leave.” Jo says, “It was a gift!” The adult revises their guess: “I didn't know. Let's protect the pieces and decide about repair later.” They promise only time they can actually offer.

## Practice

Fictional: Mina is lining up stones; departure is necessary. Compare A: “Stop being silly.” B: “Tell me every stone's name before we go.” C: “You have arranged these carefully. We need to go. Would you like to leave them like this or put them in the box?” What should the adult check before offering C?

## Answers and checking

C recognizes the activity and keeps the departure limit. A belittles it; B may create an impossible delay and demands performance. Check that both choices are safe and feasible; don't offer to preserve a display someone must immediately clear away. Mina may still be upset. A respectful response is not proved by quick compliance.

## Try independently

Write an invitation for a fictional child who wants to explain a game you do not understand. Include an honest time limit and room to decline.
