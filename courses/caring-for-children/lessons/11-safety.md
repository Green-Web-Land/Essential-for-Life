# 11. Protect safety and seek help

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Recognize when ordinary conversation is not enough.

## Explanation

Safety remains the adult's responsibility. A kind explanation does not physically remove a hazard. Use suitable supervision, secure dangerous items and current local product/safety guidance. Online, agree age-appropriate boundaries and a route to a trusted adult; a child's promise to “be careful” is not a technical safeguard.

If there is immediate danger or a medical emergency, contact local emergency services; in Canada call 9-1-1 where available. [Canadian emergency guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/campaigns/get-help-here.html). Do not wait for a course reply, GitHub issue or chatbot.

Ontario's official reporting guide says reasonable grounds to suspect a child who is or appears under 16 needs protection require an immediate direct report to a children's aid society; concerns about 16- or 17-year-olds may also be reported. This is Ontario-specific, not a worldwide rule. Use the [official reporting guide](https://files.ontario.ca/pdf-3/mccss-report-child-abuse-and-neglect-en-2022-03-31.pdf) for the complete responsibilities. Do not conduct your own investigation or wait for proof before seeking the required help.

Listen without interrogating, promising secrecy or asking a child to reenact an experience. This lesson does not provide a personalized safeguarding plan.

## Worked example

Fictional: a child begins describing something that makes the caregiver concerned about safety. The caregiver listens, does not suggest answers, and uses the appropriate child-protection route. They do not paste the account into a public forum to ask strangers whether it “counts.”

## Practice

Sort: A. disagreement about a harmless game; B. immediate danger; C. reasonable grounds for a protection concern in Ontario involving a child under 16. Which requires emergency help, which requires the direct protection report, and which may be an everyday conversation?

## Answers and checking

A may be handled through ordinary listening and limits. B requires local emergency help. C requires the direct report described above; immediate danger can require both emergency and protection help. These are supplied categories, not a diagnostic test for deciding a real case.

## Try independently

Identify the official emergency and child-protection contact source for your region without submitting a report or entering anyone's personal information. If reviewing this topic is upsetting, skip without explanation.
