# 4. Take thoughts and feelings seriously

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Acknowledge emotion while leaving room for correction and safe limits.

## Explanation

A child does not have to persuade you that a feeling is reasonable before you listen. Your interpretation may be wrong: “You seem disappointed” leaves more space than “You are jealous.” A child can disagree, have several feelings, or not want a label.

Acknowledging a feeling does not approve every action. “You can be angry; I won't let the cup be thrown at someone” separates the experience from the danger. Avoid using the acknowledgment as a bargain: “I listened, so now stop crying.” You are offering attention, not buying a result.

The Canadian Paediatric Society recommends acknowledging young children's feelings and setting developmentally appropriate expectations. [Source](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children). The wording below is an authored example, not a clinical protocol.

## Worked example

Fictional: Bea says, “You never listen!” The adult could argue over “never.” Instead: “Something felt missed. I can listen now.” Bea says, “Not now.” The adult can respect that and offer a later opportunity, while still attending to necessary care. No one forces Bea to explain or hug.

## Practice

Fictional: Lee wants to hit another child after a disagreement. Compare “Nice children don't feel angry” with “You're angry. Hitting isn't safe. Let's make space.” Improve the second sentence so it doesn't claim to know Lee's feelings with certainty.

## Answers and checking

“You seem angry; tell me if I've got that wrong. Hitting isn't safe. Let's make space.” The adult must actually protect the children; words alone are not a safeguard. The first sentence shames a feeling and gives no safe next action. Do not use the exercise as instruction in physical restraint.

## Try independently

Write a response to “I'm worried but don't want to tell you why.” Allow privacy while making clear that help remains available.
