# 6. Set kind, clear boundaries

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Make a necessary limit understandable and carry it out without humiliation.

## Explanation

A useful limit describes what the adult will protect and what can happen next. Offer choices only when both are real. “Choose now or I won't love you” is a threat, not a choice. Avoid hitting, ridicule and humiliation; the Canadian Paediatric Society advises against physical punishment and shaming. [Source](https://caringforkids.cps.ca/handouts/behavior-and-development/positive-discipline-for-young-children).

Not every preference needs a limit. Ask whether you are protecting safety, a necessary commitment, another person's rights, or simply your preferred style. Explain briefly and follow through proportionately. A child can dislike a limit without losing your care.

Do not demand affection as payment for kindness. A wave or words can replace a hug. Necessary care may still need to happen; explain, preserve dignity, and seek appropriate help when it becomes difficult rather than treating force as the lesson's method.

![Acknowledge; Protect; Offer](../images/limits.png)

Text equivalent: Acknowledge: You seem disappointed; Allow correction. Protect: We still need to leave; Care stays with the adult. Offer: Shelf or suitable box?; Only real, safe choices.

## Worked example

Fictional: Noor wants a loud game while another person sleeps. “We need quiet here. We can play a quiet game or use the other room with me.” If the other room is unavailable, it is not an honest option. The adult helps carry out the feasible choice instead of repeating “be good.”

## Practice

Fictional: leaving the playground is necessary. Evaluate A: “Walk with me or I'll leave you.” B: “We are leaving now. Would you like to carry the bag or have me carry it?” C: “Stay as long as you want,” when the adult cannot allow that.

## Answers and checking

B gives a small real choice within the departure. It does not make departure optional. A threatens abandonment; C creates a false promise. If the child cannot manage the transition, reassess what assistance is safe and appropriate; don't demand adult emotional control.

## Try independently

Draft a boundary about borrowing someone else's belongings. Include care for the disappointed child and respect for the owner's refusal.
