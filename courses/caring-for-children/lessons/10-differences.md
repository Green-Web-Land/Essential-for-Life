# 10. Support different needs

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Objective

Adapt help without turning differences into labels or lower respect.

## Explanation

The same task can present different barriers: language, noise, mobility, unfamiliar routines, sensory discomfort or lack of understanding. Ask what helps, observe what changes and use the child's established supports. Do not assume a quiet child understands less or an articulate child needs no assistance.

CDC advises discussing developmental concerns with a child's doctor. [Source](https://www.cdc.gov/concerned). Its service routes are US-specific; local Canadian services differ. A course exercise or chatbot description cannot diagnose a child. Do not postpone a concern merely because this course says children vary.

A useful request for help describes observations and context, without publishing the child's record. Share relevant information privately with the appropriate professional. Avoid assigning a motive such as laziness when the actual obstacle is unknown. The goal is access and understanding, not making every child communicate identically.

## Worked example

Fictional: Rey follows a picture sequence but struggles with spoken instructions in a busy room. The caregiver uses the pictures and asks what else would help. They do not conclude Rey cannot reason or diagnose why the pictures help. If concerns persist, they discuss the observations with an appropriate professional.

## Practice

Fictional: “Tess doesn't answer quickly in the group, but answers later in writing.” Choose a useful next step: A. force a timed spoken answer; B. offer the written option and time; C. announce a diagnosis; D. assume nothing could ever need assessment.

## Answers and checking

B uses the supplied evidence to remove a barrier. A may test speed rather than understanding. C lacks evidence. D is also unsupported: practical adaptations and seeking help for concerns can coexist.

## Try independently

Write a private, fictional help request containing one observation, the setting and one question. Do not add identifying details or an invented diagnosis.
