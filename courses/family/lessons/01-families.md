# 1. Families, care and differences

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Separate stated facts from assumptions about a household.

Adults 18+. All people and situations below are fictional. Work on paper; skip or replace any scenario you do not want to read. No personal disclosure or real conversation is required.

## Understand

A family may include relatives, partners, adoptive or foster connections, or people who regard one another as family. For these exercises, use the relationships the scenario supplies. Do not decide who counts by surname, gender, marriage or whether people live together.

A role describes an arrangement; it does not tell us what someone can do, wants to do or owes in every situation. When planning an ordinary shared activity, list the work, ask about capacity and agree who will do what. A familiar custom can be discussed without dismissing its meaning to someone else. This course does not determine legal duties.

Use three columns: **known**, **unknown**, and **question**. Keep an explanation such as “They do not care” out of the known column unless it is actually a stated fact—and even someone's statement does not let us read another person's mind.

## Worked example

Mina lives with her adult brother Dev and their aunt Jo. Dev works evenings. Jo prefers a written shopping list. Mina says, “We have no agreed shopping day.”

Known: Dev's work timing, Jo's preference and the missing agreement. Unknown: who can travel to a shop or pay for purchases. Useful question: “What part of shopping could each of us take on this week?” Assigning Jo all shopping because she is older adds an unsupported assumption.

## Practise

Ari and their partner Sam live apart. Ari says, “I can visit on Saturday but cannot drive.” Sam wants help moving two boxes. Write two known facts, two unknowns and one question that avoids assigning a role by gender.

## Suggested reasoning

Known: Ari's stated availability and inability to drive. Unknown: how heavy the boxes are and whether another transport option is available. A useful question is, “Could we first check what needs moving and what help is practical?” Living apart does not establish how much either person cares. Other questions earn credit if they seek missing information without inventing motives.

## Try a new situation

A cousin declines a holiday invitation without giving a reason. Write one fact and one neutral response. Check: the refusal is known; its cause is not. “Thanks for letting me know” does not demand an explanation.
