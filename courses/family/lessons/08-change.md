# 8. Support through change

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Offer bounded help that the recipient can accept, alter or decline.

Adults 18+. All people and situations below are fictional. Work on paper; skip or replace any scenario you do not want to read. No personal disclosure or real conversation is required.

## Understand

A change in work, housing or care arrangements may change what support is useful. Instead of deciding what another adult needs, offer a concrete option and ask. Separate the help you can provide from the help you hope somebody else can supply.

A support offer should state its limits. “I can bring groceries Thursday” differs from “I will handle everything.” Do not promise availability that you cannot provide. The recipient may prefer a different form of help or no help from you.

This lesson is about arranging ordinary practical assistance. It does not teach treatment, medical care tasks or legal decision-making on someone's behalf. Where those are needed, the relevant qualified service should explain the options.

## Worked example

After moving, Rowan says unpacking is tiring and requests help labelling boxes. Friend Cam offers one hour Saturday and asks whether written labels or colour markers would be useful. Rowan chooses written labels. Cam does not reorganize private papers or contact Rowan's employer. The accepted task sets the scope.

## Practise

Dana's shift changes next week. Their adult sibling offers to “take over all your appointments.” Rewrite this as a specific offer that leaves Dana in charge. Include timing and a question about what information, if any, Dana wants to share.

## Suggested reasoning

For example: “I can spend half an hour Sunday helping you list appointments, if you want. Would that help? You can choose which dates to share.” This is an offer, not permission to log in, cancel appointments or speak for Dana. Another bounded task can also work.

## Try a new situation

A fictional carer says they can no longer do a weekly errand. Draft a response that recognises the limit and identifies a next planning question without assigning blame. Do not assume another relative can automatically take over.
