# 6. Handle everyday disagreements

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Choose a proportionate next step without treating intimidation as ordinary conflict.

Adults 18+. All people and situations below are fictional. Work on paper; skip or replace any scenario you do not want to read. No personal disclosure or real conversation is required.

## Understand

For an ordinary disagreement where people can speak freely, separate the decision from personal attacks. Write the issue in one sentence, identify each stated preference and compare a small number of workable options. A pause can be useful when a safe conversation is becoming unproductive; a future discussion is an invitation, not an obligation.

The examples below are editorial practice, not a clinical test. Threats, intimidation or restrictions on someone's freedom are not solved by giving everyone an equal share of blame. The Public Health Agency of Canada describes family violence as including controlling and threatening behaviour, not only physical harm. See Sources and lesson 9.

Do not use a compromise exercise to negotiate whether someone may threaten or harm another person. A lack of a reported threat in a short story also does not prove a real situation is safe.

## Worked example

In this fictional ordinary disagreement, Nia wants a quiet Saturday morning and Sol wants to practise music. Both are free to refuse suggestions; neither threatens the other. They compare headphones, another room, or practice after lunch. A trial after lunch is useful only if both agree. No option can be declared best without checking constraints.

## Practise

Version A: Two adults disagree about a television programme and agree they may leave the room. Version B: One blocks the doorway and threatens the other for trying to leave. Explain why the same “take turns choosing” answer does not fit both.

## Suggested reasoning

A may be suitable for ordinary option comparison. B contains a threat and blocked movement; treating it as a programming dispute overlooks the stated danger. Stop the joint communication exercise and choose the support route in lesson 9. Do not diagnose the person's personality or ask the threatened person to be more persuasive.

## Try a new situation

For a safe fictional disagreement about a visit time, list two options and one unanswered practical question. Check that both people retain the ability to decline.
