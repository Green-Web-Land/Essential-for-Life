# Facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Ask what the learner wants to do and which words are unfamiliar. Use the fictional examples here rather than personal messages, financial records or workplace documents. Offer paper practice before a device demonstration. Avoid assumptions about age, disability, language or previous opportunities to learn.

## A repeatable session

1. State the lesson objective in plain language.
2. Read or describe the example and diagram; its text equivalent covers the same concept.
3. Let the learner attempt the practice before reading the suggested reasoning.
4. Ask “What did you observe?” and “How would you check?” rather than doing the task for them.
5. Try the transfer question and record what needs more practice.

Provide larger text, breaks or spoken descriptions as useful. Keyboard, pointer and touch preferences vary. Do not change accessibility settings without the learner's agreement. No diagnosis or disclosure of a disability is needed to participate.

## Device boundaries

Use a device the learner is allowed to use. For lesson 5, identify an appropriate practice folder whose sharing/sync behaviour is understood. If this is unclear, use paper. Never request passwords, recovery codes or administrator access. Stop at an overwrite warning, unexpected account request or unfamiliar download. There is no required software installation.

Keep assessment informal: ask the learner to explain their choice and demonstrate with fictional files. A paper response does not prove a device skill. Do not collect or upload practice work by default; learners can retain or discard their own fictional worksheets. No learner trial or accessibility certification has been performed for this edition.
