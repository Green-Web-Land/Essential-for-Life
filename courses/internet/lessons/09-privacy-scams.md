# 9. Privacy and deceptive requests

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

**Learning goal:** Recognise an unsafe request and explain what private browsing does not do.

Private browsing primarily changes what a browser retains locally after the session. It does not make you anonymous to websites, a network operator or an Internet provider. Signing in still identifies you to that service. Downloads and bookmarks can remain. The exact local behaviour depends on the browser.

Scams often use urgency, authority or a promised reward to hurry a decision. Those are warning signs, not proof that every urgent message is fraudulent. Look at the requested action: revealing a password, giving a code, installing remote-control software or sending money changes the risk. Stop and verify independently before acting.

Do not practise by opening suspicious links. If an incident affects a real account, use the service's established help and recovery route from a trusted device when possible. If an organisation owns the device or account, contact its support through a known channel. Do not grant access to an unverified caller claiming to be support.

Minimising shared information is useful even when a service is legitimate. Ask what it needs, why, who can see it and whether a less revealing route meets the task. Once another person has a copy, you cannot assume that deleting your copy removes theirs.

## Worked example

A fictional popup claims Mina must call a number immediately to repair “Internet damage.” She stops interacting with the popup and uses a separately known support route. The alarming words are not a diagnosis.

## Try it — paper is enough

A friend says, “Use private browsing and upload the group contact list; nobody can identify us.” Identify two problems and propose a lower-disclosure alternative.

## Answer and reasoning

Private browsing is not anonymity, and uploading other people's details exposes them regardless of browser mode. Ask the organiser a general question without the list, using a verified route. No upload is needed in the exercise.

## Use it somewhere else

Write a pause-and-verify card in three steps: stop the requested action, establish an independent route, decide what minimum information is needed.
