# Curriculum — Everyday Safety and Getting Help

Audience: adult beginners 18+. Aim: make a reasoned next-step choice without treating a short course as specialist training. No prerequisite or real-world exercise.

| Lesson | Demonstration of learning |
|---|---|
| [Notice a possible hazard](lessons/01-notice.md) | Separate what you observe from what you assume. |
| [Pause without delaying urgent help](lessons/02-next-step.md) | Distinguish stopping a routine activity from delaying emergency help. |
| [Everyday spaces and equipment](lessons/03-spaces.md) | Recognise a concern without attempting a hazardous fix. |
| [Fire, smoke and household substances](lessons/04-fire-substances.md) | Recognise when specialist or emergency help is needed. |
| [Outside, travel and changing conditions](lessons/05-outside.md) | Read a notice without treating it as a complete safety assessment. |
| [Pressure, boundaries and personal safety](lessons/06-boundaries.md) | Recognise coercion and identify support without assuming one response fits everyone. |
| [Suspicious messages and requests](lessons/07-messages.md) | Verify a claimed identity through a separate established route. |
| [Choose the right help route](lessons/08-help.md) | Match a fictional need to the service's purpose and location. |
| [Explain what happened](lessons/09-explain.md) | Give concrete information without inventing a cause. |
| [Make a safer decision](lessons/10-decisions.md) | Combine observation, action, help and uncertainty. |

## How to use the course

Read the explanation, consider the worked case, answer before checking the suggested reasoning, then try the changed fact or transfer task. Revisiting a distinction is welcome. There is no required pace or pass certificate. Alternatives to emergency and coercion cases are supplied; skipping is allowed without explanation.

The final lesson combines six tasks. The facilitator rubric supports feedback on explanations, not predictions about how a learner will behave during an emergency. Suitable alternative reasoning is accepted when it respects the supplied facts and avoids adding danger.

## Boundaries

Only fictional cards, text and diagrams are required. No inspection, electrical work, chemical use, travel experiment, stranger contact or practice call. No personal records, addresses or family experiences are requested. Named help services are geographically limited examples with dated source notes. The course cannot classify every incident.
