# Facilitator guide

Use the supplied cards, never participants' personal emergencies. State in advance that this is discussion-based preparation, not a practical drill or professional training. Do not surprise learners with alarms, simulated calls, threatening messages or staged hazards. A learner may skip, pause or use a neutral alternative without giving a reason.

## Conduct

Offer reading aloud, large print, typed or spoken responses and additional time as needed. Read image text equivalents. Do not assume those options establish formal accessibility conformance. Avoid scoring confidence, speed, accent or a learner's willingness to disclose. No compulsory public answer or grade.

Discuss uncertainty explicitly. Ask “Which supplied fact supports that?” and “What changed?” Do not reward an invented diagnosis or a claim that an action guarantees safety. Correct any proposal to investigate a fire, test damaged equipment, mix chemicals or contact a suspected offender as part of the class.

If someone raises a real current danger, stop the exercise and prioritise appropriate real help. Do not turn it into a classroom case or promise secrecy you cannot honour. For actual disclosures, use applicable organisational safeguarding procedures and qualified local support; this guide does not establish your legal obligations. Do not record identifying details in the course repository.

## Six-task feedback rubric

For each task in Lesson 10:
- 2: uses the supplied fact, gives an appropriate next step and explains the requested distinction without adding danger.
- 1: identifies part of the answer but omits a relevant explanation or limit; discuss and revise.
- 0: does not yet show the distinction or proposes an action adding danger; provide feedback and another paper attempt.

Six tasks × 2 gives a maximum of 12. This is optional learning feedback, not a safety risk score, qualification or pass threshold. Accept defensible alternatives. A learner using the neutral packet is assessed on the same observation, contact and changed-fact skills.
