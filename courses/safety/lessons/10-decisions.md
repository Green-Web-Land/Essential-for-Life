# 10. Make a safer decision

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Goal

Combine observation, action, help and uncertainty.

## Understand

Use only the supplied fictional cards. This is learning feedback, not a measure of real-world safety competence. More than one response may be reasonable when you explain the circumstances. A high score cannot certify a person, building, device or plan.

The skill is to avoid adding danger, select appropriate support and notice when facts change. Do not guess a medical diagnosis, fire cause or someone's intentions from a short story. [Help-route sources](../SOURCES.md)

## Worked example

Worked card D0: “A message claims to be from an account provider and demands a secret code.” A strong answer withholds the code, uses an independently established contact route and states that the sender is unknown. A weak answer treats a logo as proof. This demonstrates reasoning, not an actual fraud investigation.

## Paper activity

Complete six tasks on paper:

1. D1: Already outside Practice Hall in Toronto, you see flames inside. Name an observed fact and immediate next step.
2. D1: Name the help route and one action that would add danger.
3. D2: No immediate danger; a fictional Ontario resident wants community meal information. Select a route and say what a referral does not guarantee.
4. D3: A message demands an access code. Explain how to verify separately.
5. D4: A fictional person may have swallowed cleaner and is awake, with no severe symptom stated. Choose prompt help. Changed fact: unconsciousness. Explain the changed route.
6. D1: Occupancy is unknown. Draft a brief account without inventing this fact or going inside to check.

Alternative packet: replace Tasks 1, 2 and 6 with the closed-path notice O1 from Lesson 5: identify observation/action, responsible contact and a concise uncertainty statement. Replace Task 5 with O2, the later whole-park closure, explaining how the new information changes the plan. No personal disclosure or drill.

## Suggested reasoning

1. Flames are observed; seek emergency help from safety.
2. 911; re-entering would add danger.
3. 211; referral does not guarantee a place or eligibility.
4. Use a separately established genuine provider route, not the message's link or number; do not send the code.
5. Prompt Ontario Poison Centre advice; unconsciousness changes the response to 911 immediately.
6. State fictional location, observation and unknown occupancy; follow responder questions without investigating.

Alternative answers: O1 closes the north path until a new notice; do not enter it, ask the practice park office, and do not assume another path is safe. O2 closes the whole park, so the proposed visit must change. See the [facilitator rubric](../FACILITATOR-GUIDE.md).

## Try the distinction again

Name one distinction you want to remember: observation versus guess, urgent help versus routine enquiry, or genuine contact versus a claimed identity. Explain it using one supplied card. No public score, personal incident report or certificate is required.
