# 5. Outside, travel and changing conditions

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Goal

Read a notice without treating it as a complete safety assessment.

## Understand

A weather alert is more than a coloured heading. Read its full text: affected place, timing, expected impacts and advised actions. Follow current official directions. An old screenshot may not describe conditions now. [Source S9](../SOURCES.md)

A route being open on an old map does not establish that it is usable today. The fictional notice below teaches reading, not route planning. It is not an official alert and must never be used as one.

## Worked example

Fictional notice O1: “Practice Park Office. Issued Monday at 09:00. North path CLOSED until a new notice because of fallen branches. Do not enter. Ask the park office about current access.” A map printed the previous month draws that path normally. Neither document names a safe alternative.

## Paper activity

Copy four items from O1: issuing body, time, affected place and requested action. Does “until a new notice” mean the path opens automatically tomorrow? Does the old map override the notice? No outdoor trip is required.

## Suggested reasoning

Practice Park Office; Monday 09:00; north path; do not enter and ask the office about access. There is no automatic tomorrow opening. The old map does not override the supplied closure. The exercise gives no evidence for choosing another path; “I need current information” is a complete response.

## Try the distinction again

Changed fact O2: a later notice closes the whole practice park. Explain how that changes the proposed visit. Do not invent a shortcut or cross a real barrier to test the lesson.
