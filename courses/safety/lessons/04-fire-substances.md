# 4. Fire, smoke and household substances

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Goal

Recognise when specialist or emergency help is needed.

## Understand

For the fire example, you are already outside. Do not re-enter a burning building for possessions or to investigate. Contact emergency help from safety and follow responders. Indoor escape choices depend on the building and circumstances; this lesson is not an evacuation plan. [Source S6](../SOURCES.md)

Do not mix household chemical products. Follow their labels; do not taste or deliberately smell an unknown substance to identify it. For suspected harmful exposure, seek poison advice promptly. [Source S7](../SOURCES.md) In Ontario, the Poison Centre route is in Lesson 8. If someone is unconscious or having trouble breathing, call 911 immediately. [Source S5](../SOURCES.md) Do not wait for symptoms to become severe before seeking poison advice.

## Worked example

Fictional case F1: Ari is already outside the home; smoke and flames are visible inside. A bag is still inside. Ari stays out and calls emergency help. Fictional case C1: someone may have swallowed household cleaner and is awake; no severe symptom is stated. The appropriate next step is prompt poison advice, not a homemade remedy or a prediction that everything is fine.

## Paper activity

For F1 explain why retrieving the bag is not a safer choice. For C1 choose between prompt poison advice and waiting to see what happens. Changed fact C2: the person becomes unconscious. What changes? Do not act out these cases.

## Suggested reasoning

F1: re-entry adds exposure to danger. C1: prompt poison advice; missing symptoms do not establish harmlessness. C2: immediate emergency response, not a routine queue. Follow the professional's instructions; these notes do not provide treatment. Do not create an exposure or gather a container if doing so adds danger or delays urgent help.

## Try the distinction again

Alternative non-distressing activity: a fictional unopened cleaning product has an unreadable label. Explain why you would leave it unused and ask the manufacturer or responsible person for information, instead of guessing a mixture.
