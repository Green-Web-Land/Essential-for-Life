# 6. Pressure, boundaries and personal safety

[Course home](../README.md) · [Curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md)

## Goal

Recognise coercion and identify support without assuming one response fits everyone.

## Understand

Pressure to keep harmful conduct secret, hand over private material or obey threats is a reason to seek support. Knowing someone does not prove that they are safe to confide in. Choose support with regard to your situation; a qualified local service can help consider options. There is no requirement here to confront someone, announce plans or leave at a particular moment.

For immediate danger in Canada, call 911. For non-immediate support, the federal family violence directory and Ontario community referral route can help locate services. [Source S10](../SOURCES.md) A monitored device can expose help-seeking; deleting browser history does not guarantee privacy. Seek a safer way to contact support when possible. [Source S11](../SOURCES.md) This lesson cannot assess personal safeguarding circumstances.

## Worked example

Fictional case B1: an acquaintance demands private messages and threatens consequences if Ren talks to anyone. We do not know what contact method is safe for Ren. A reasonable answer names a specialist support option and this uncertainty, instead of ordering Ren to confront the acquaintance. Ren is not responsible for the other person's threats.

## Paper activity

Without sharing your experiences, identify one pressure signal in B1 and one fact needed to choose a safer contact method. You may replace B1 with this non-distressing case B2: a club organiser requests an immediate answer to an optional invitation; Pat would prefer time to consider it.

## Suggested reasoning

B1: threatened consequences and enforced secrecy are pressure signals. Whether a device is monitored matters. Appropriate support can help consider a plan; the class cannot prescribe it. B2: Pat can request time or decline if that is comfortable; the card does not establish abuse or an emergency. Different situations need different responses.

## Try the distinction again

Write one respectful facilitator sentence: “You may use the alternative case; no personal explanation is needed.” This exercise does not require a real conversation with someone causing concern.
