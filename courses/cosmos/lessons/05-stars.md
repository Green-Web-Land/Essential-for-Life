# 5. The Sun and other stars

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Explain why stars do not all follow one identical life story.

Adults 18+. All invented records and model numbers are labelled. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

The Sun releases energy through nuclear fusion in its core. This is not a chemical fire needing air. A star changes over time; its mass strongly influences its development.

Stars form from collapsing gas and dust. During the main-sequence stage, hydrogen fusion supports long-lasting energy output. Stars of different masses can have very different lifetimes. Later paths also differ: a Sun-like star can leave a white dwarf, while some massive stars end with a neutron star or black hole after a supernova. Not every star becomes a black hole.

In this course, “life” and “death” of a star describe stages of physical change. They do not mean the star is a living organism.

## Worked example

A fictional narrator says “all stars have the Sun's lifetime and all explode the same way.” This erases differences the explanation says matter, especially mass.

## Practise

Choose the more defensible statement:
A. Every star becomes a black hole.
B. Stellar histories depend on physical properties, and the Sun's path does not describe every star.

Explain the choice. Then identify what is missing from a claim that a pictured star will end its main-sequence stage exactly next year.

## Suggested reasoning

B respects different stellar paths. A precise timing claim needs evidence about the particular object, a model and uncertainties; an attractive image supplies none of those by itself.

## Try a new situation

An artist shows a star as a smiling character. How could it be used honestly? Check: label it as an imaginative illustration and separate that character from the physical explanation.
