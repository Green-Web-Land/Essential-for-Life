# 7. Galaxies and cosmic history

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Separate evidence about the universe from a complete explanation of everything.

Adults 18+. All invented records and model numbers are labelled. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; calculator optional.

## Understand

Galaxies have varied shapes and contain stars, gas and dust. Our Milky Way is a spiral galaxy. Seeing another galaxy means receiving light that has travelled to us; distant views are not instantaneous pictures of events happening everywhere at one shared “now.”

NASA's universe overview describes an expanding, cooling early universe and an age of about 13.8 billion years. This is an approximate scientific estimate in the source checked on 11 September 2026, not an exact birthday. The cosmic microwave background is observable evidence associated with the early universe.

The Big Bang model describes cosmic development. It does not settle every question about the earliest conditions or what, if anything, preceded them. Do not interpret a sketch as a photograph taken from outside the universe.

## Worked example

Imagine a fictional nearby source whose light takes 4 years to cross an idealized fixed distance to a receiver. The received signal describes the source when that light left, not an instant update. This example ignores cosmic expansion and is not a distance recipe for very remote galaxies.

## Practise

A second fictional signal takes 6 years on the same kind of simplified account. How much longer is its delay? Does either signal tell us the source's total age?

## Suggested reasoning

The delay is 2 years longer. Light travel time and an object's age are different quantities. A source could have existed before it emitted the signal; its total age is not supplied.

## Try a new situation

A headline turns “about 13.8 billion years” into an exact date and time. What was lost? Check: approximation, evidence/model context and uncertainty.
