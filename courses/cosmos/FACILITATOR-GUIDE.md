# Exploring the Cosmos — facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Use the supplied paper activities. No telescope, Sun observation, lasers, filters, travel or experiment is part of the course. Do not turn an illustration into an equipment instruction.

## Help the learner reason

Ask what each arrow means, what units are being compared, whether a value is fictional and what facts would be needed for a stronger claim. Allow a table or spoken explanation in place of a drawing. Separate arithmetic mistakes from understanding; a calculator is optional.

Do not treat “we cannot decide from this information” as failure when the evidence is incomplete. Invite curiosity without ranking unsupported speculation as established science. A learner may revise an answer after seeing the assumptions.

## Final project rubric

Six tasks in lesson 10. For each, award 0 for missing or contradictory reasoning, 1 for partly correct reasoning with an important gap, or 2 for reasoning consistent with the supplied data. Maximum **12 points**. This is feedback, not a qualification or prediction of ability.

| Task | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| 1: scale | P/Q/R positions 2/6/14 cm; Q-to-R gap 8 cm; marker size distinguished from distance. |
| 2: drop | Absolute drop 2 units and percentage drop 2%, using baseline 100. |
| 3: timing | Low samples at 2, 6, 10; adjacent intervals 4 and 4. |
| 4: interpretation | Separates supplied repeating lows from a possible transit; confirmation not claimed. |
| 5: scope | No connection between R and the signal is supplied, and no evidence of life is supplied. |
| 6: caption | Labels the data fictional, states a correct result and a material limitation. |

Sample caption: “Fictional light samples have three lows 4 time units apart, each 2% below baseline. They do not establish a planet or its properties.”

Exhibit B is sampled, not continuous. Do not mark a learner wrong for pointing out that the full dip shape and duration are unknown. No error bars were supplied; this is not a statistical detection exercise.

## Numerical and exercise keys

- Lesson 2: 1/3/7 units at 2 cm per unit become 2/6/14 cm; Q-to-R gap 8 cm. At 1 cm per unit: 1/3/7 cm and gap 4 cm.
- Lesson 3: 12 ÷ 2 = 6 rotations; transfer 15 ÷ 3 = 5. Fixed reference directions specified; daylight length is not given.
- Lesson 4: Sun/star, Earth/planet, Earth's Moon/natural satellite, Pluto/dwarf planet.
- Lesson 6: full toy sample matches A at 2/5/8. Partial sample 2/5 lacks the third supplied position; not a confirmed physical identification.
- Lesson 7: 6 − 4 = 2 years difference in fictional light delay; neither value supplies total object age.
- Lesson 8: baseline 100 to 98 is 2%; baseline 200 to 198 is 1%.
- Lesson 10 miniature: 50 to 49 is 2%; times 1/4/7 have intervals 3/3.
- Lesson 10 transfer: positions 4/8/16 cm; baseline 200 to 198 is 1%; times 3/8/13 have intervals 5/5.

Accept other well-supported explanations of missing evidence. No independent scientific review, learner pilot or formal accessibility assessment has been performed.
