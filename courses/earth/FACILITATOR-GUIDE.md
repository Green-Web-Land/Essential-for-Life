# How Earth Works — facilitator guide

[Course home](README.md)

Keep the learning on paper using the supplied fiction. Do not request fieldwork, samples, digging, climbing, handling organisms or personal environmental information.

## Facilitation

Read the objective, explain the example, allow an answer and compare the reasoning. Ask what an arrow represents, what the unit is, what boundary is used and what remains unknown. A correctly identified limitation is a useful result.

Provide larger text, a spoken response or a table instead of an image when needed. Learners may skip or replace an example with another supplied fictional task. Do not treat an arithmetic error as evidence of low ability; distinguish calculation from scientific interpretation.

Keep the metaphor explicit: Earth supports life and has interacting processes; it is not called an organism. Avoid implying natural systems always return to a perfect state. Do not use a global climate explanation to predict one person's weather or exposure.

## Final exercise rubric

Lesson 10 has six tasks. Score each 0 for missing or contradictory reasoning, 1 for partly supported reasoning with an important gap, or 2 for clear reasoning consistent with the supplied account. Maximum **12 points**. This is learning feedback, not a qualification or judgement of environmental expertise.

| Task | Evidence for 2 points |
|---|---|
| 1: budget | Both sums equal 100; B–A differences are −10, +20, −10 and sum to zero. |
| 2: claims | Selects claim 2 and rejects unsupported cause, flood or water-safety inferences. |
| 3: pathways | Supplies two meaningful routes with arrow meanings and no compulsory universal path. |
| 4: missing facts | Names three relevant absent measurements, conditions or account details. |
| 5: storage | Distinguishes storage increase from final storage; initial amount is unknown. |
| 6: report | Separates supplied output, a possible question and limits without inventing observations. |

Accept other well-supported answers. Scenario C balances at 30 + 60 + 30 = 120; its input differs from A, so a landscape-only explanation is not established.

## Other numerical keys

- Energy: +5 units for the first invented account, −5 for the second.
- Soil: initial solids 60, spaces 40; revised water 30 and air 10, total 100.
- Five-day temperatures: first mean 12 °C; second 10 °C; difference 2 °C.
- Carbon: final stores 44 and 45; second net change −5.
- Lesson 9: storage increase 15; the inconsistent account exceeds input by 5.
- Lesson 10 mini-example: increase 8, final storage 18.

Ask learners to explain assumptions as well as numbers. None of these values is an empirical measurement or local prediction.

No learner pilot, independent scientific review or formal accessibility conformance assessment. Refer actual water-safety, disaster or environmental-assessment questions to appropriate qualified services rather than deciding from the exercise.
