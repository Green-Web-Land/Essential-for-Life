# 8. Carbon and human influence

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Separate a carbon store from a transfer and a global claim from a local forecast.

Adults 18+. All named places, scenarios and numbers in exercises are fictional. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

Carbon is present in air, oceans, living things, soils and rocks. NASA's carbon-cycle explanation describes transfers such as photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and exchanges involving oceans and geological stores. A reservoir is a store; a transfer is movement between stores over time.

Burning fossil fuels moves carbon from long-stored material into the atmosphere, principally as carbon dioxide. Land-use changes also affect carbon stores and transfers. NASA's current causes page, checked 11 September 2026, identifies human greenhouse-gas emissions as the principal driver of recent global warming. This is a global scientific conclusion, not a weather forecast for a particular street.

The natural greenhouse effect and its human-driven strengthening are distinct ideas. Greenhouse gases affect infrared energy exchanges; carbon dioxide is not the same thing as sunlight. Avoid treating a planting action as an immediate, exact cancellation of any emission without a full account.

## Worked example

In a purely invented carbon store, 40 carbon units are present initially, 7 enter and 3 leave during one interval. The final store is 44 units. The numbers are not a climate model, emissions estimate or offset claim.

## Practise

For another fictional store, begin with 50 units, add 4 and remove 9. Compute the final store and net change. Explain why a large store does not automatically mean a large yearly transfer.

## Suggested reasoning

The final store is 45 units and the net change is −5. A stored amount and a transfer per interval are different quantities. Their relationship needs timing and process information.

## Try a new situation

A headline uses the long-term global warming conclusion to promise the temperature at one address next month. Identify the unsupported leap. Check: global attribution does not by itself supply a local short-term prediction.
