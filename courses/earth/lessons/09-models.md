# 9. Reading evidence and models

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Separate a model output, an observation and missing information.

Adults 18+. All named places, scenarios and numbers in exercises are fictional. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

A model selects features for a purpose. A map shows spatial relationships at a chosen scale; a budget follows inputs, outputs and storage within a defined boundary and interval. Neither reproduces every part of the real system.

Ask where values came from, which assumptions were made and which measurements could check the result. A neatly balanced fictional budget can teach accounting without validating a hydrological model.

For the course's simplified water accounts, storage change equals input minus output. We state which transfers count as outputs and exclude internal movements from that sum. Do not count the same water twice.

## Worked example

Fictional box: 60 water units enter, 20 leave to the atmosphere and 25 leave through an outlet. Storage increases by 15. Movement from soil to plants inside the box is not another external output; the plant-to-atmosphere transfer may be one.

## Practise

An invented diagram lists 80 units input, 30 atmospheric output, 40 outlet output and 15 storage increase. Does it balance? What should you report?

## Suggested reasoning

30 + 40 + 15 = 85, which exceeds input by 5. Report an accounting discrepancy; ask about missing inputs, signs, timing or double counting. Do not silently change a number to make it fit.

## Try a new situation

The maker calls a watershed sketch “not to scale.” Can you measure a real distance from it? Check: no; use a suitable scaled map or actual measurement for that task.
