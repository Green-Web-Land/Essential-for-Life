# 5. Soil and life

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Describe soil as a varied mixture with several functions.

Adults 18+. All named places, scenarios and numbers in exercises are fictional. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

NRCS describes soils in terms of minerals and organic matter, liquids and gases, with properties shaped by environment and organisms over time. Soil supports life and influences water movement and nutrient cycling.

The spaces between solid particles can contain air or water. Composition and structure differ among soils and conditions. A classroom mixture is not a universal recipe. A soil's colour alone does not establish fertility, contamination or suitability for a crop.

Living roots and other organisms interact with the material around them. Avoid treating soil simply as ground-up rock or assuming every place has the same layer arrangement.

## Worked example

An invented 100-volume-unit soil model contains 50 mineral units, 10 organic units, 25 water units and 15 air units. The solids total 60; the air and water spaces total 40. These are labelled teaching values, not a typical-soil prescription.

## Practise

In this model only, 5 water units replace 5 air units while total volume stays 100. Give the new water and air amounts and verify the total. Explain what was assumed.

## Suggested reasoning

Water becomes 30 units and air 10; 50 + 10 + 30 + 10 = 100. The exercise fixes the solids and total volume and assumes water replaces air. Real changes need not follow that simplified setup.

## Try a new situation

Two soils share the same colour. Write two questions before claiming they hold water equally well. Check: ask about structure, composition or measured behaviour rather than inferring from colour.
