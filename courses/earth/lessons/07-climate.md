# 7. Weather and climate

[Course home](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Goal:** Choose evidence appropriate to the timescale of a claim.

Adults 18+. All named places, scenarios and numbers in exercises are fictional. Work on paper or an accessible equivalent; a calculator is optional.

## Understand

Weather describes atmospheric conditions over shorter periods; climate concerns longer-term patterns and variation. NOAA explains the distinction and the use of multi-decade records. A few local days cannot establish or refute a global long-term trend.

Climate evidence can include temperature, precipitation and other measurements across time and locations. Compare records with compatible units, methods, locations and time intervals. A chart should identify what was measured and what the average represents.

A cold day and a warming long-term climate are not contradictory descriptions of the same timescale. This lesson does not make a forecast for your location.

## Worked example

A fictional five-day temperature list is 10, 12, 14, 12, 12 °C. The mean is 12 °C. It describes those five days; calling it the town's long-term climate would be unsupported.

## Practise

Another invented five-day list is 8, 10, 12, 10, 10 °C. Find its mean and compare it with the first. Can these two lists establish a climate trend?

## Suggested reasoning

The second mean is 10 °C, 2 °C lower than the first. These brief fictional records lack the long-term context and coverage needed for a climate-trend claim. They are not real observations.

## Try a new situation

A graph mixes monthly means from one station with daily maxima from another. What should be checked? Check: location, time scale and quantity differ; do not treat the series as directly comparable.
